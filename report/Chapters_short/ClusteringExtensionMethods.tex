\section{Patient-Level Clustering Extension}\label{sec:extension_methods}

An exploratory extension was implemented on 15 October 2026 to assess whether the historical severity bands concealed reproducible subgroups under different representations, missing-data assumptions and sampling units. It supplements H1--H4 rather than redefining their original tests. The motivating literature was restricted to articles published between 15 October 2025 and 15 October 2026. Incomplete-data mixture modeling, multiple-imputation ensembles, clusterability diagnostics, and longitudinal comparison were informed by \textcite{tong2025mixturemissing,tomo2026ensemble,neville2026clusterability,julia2026longitudinal}. Evidence on UMAP distortions motivated evaluation in measured feature space \parencite{chen2026umapartifacts}. These articles motivate methodological comparisons; they do not validate specific ABI subtypes or establish that a proposed method will outperform the historical pipeline.

\subsection{Cohort and Discovery--Validation Split}

The eligible cohort comprises 17{,}406 assessments from 7{,}285 patients across the 14 retained cognitive tests. Patient identifiers link repeated observations, with no duplicate patient--assessment-time pairs. A verified injury-episode identifier was unavailable. One patient had inconsistent recorded age at injury and was excluded from trajectory modeling.

The cross-sectional analysis uses each patient's earliest eligible assessment, ordered by time since injury, with source-row order as a deterministic tie-breaker. A fixed random split (seed 20261015) allocated 5{,}828 patients to discovery and 1{,}457 to validation. Each patient contributes one index profile; therefore, no patient appears in both sets. This estimates structure in patient index profiles, whereas the historical analysis describes assessment states across follow-up. The 14-variable selection was inherited from the historical full cohort, so the split does not constitute an independent validation of feature selection.

Eligibility requires at least one observed retained test. A separate sensitivity analysis retained index assessments with at least seven observed tests to assess the influence of sparsely measured profiles.

\subsection{Shared Preprocessing and Feature Representations}

Discovery observations determine each test's 1st and 99th percentiles, direction, mean and standard deviation. Values are clipped to these limits, the timed \texttt{ATMTA} score is reversed, and tests are standardized so that larger values indicate better performance. Missing entries remain missing during this transformation. For the completed-data branches, a bounded Bayesian chained-equations imputer is fitted on discovery patients and applied to validation patients. The deterministic reference uses 15 iterations; a 50-iteration sensitivity checks convergence. This imputer is distinct from the historical \texttt{miceforest} implementation and retains its own modeling assumptions.

Completed standardized tests are combined into six domains using administration-frequency weights learned from discovery patients and normalized within each domain. The reference distance representation uses domain medians and interquartile ranges fitted on discovery patients (robust scaling). Comparators use the 14 completed tests, the five non-orientation domains, and the five-dimensional PCA basis after removing the first discovery-derived component. The residual representation is an exploratory profile-shape diagnostic; it may remove genuine signal as well as shared variation.

An additional scaling sensitivity, introduced after inspection of discovery-set variances, standardizes every domain to unit variance. It assesses the influence of ceiling-heavy domains with narrow central ranges. Comparisons of identical label sets in both geometries distinguish changes in the partition from changes in how silhouette is measured. All transformations applied to validation patients remain fixed at their discovery estimates.

The patient-level analysis consistently applies clipping, direction alignment and test standardization before aggregation. Earlier supplementary multiple-imputation and complete-case comparisons used raw-test aggregation. Their quoted results therefore describe a different preprocessing specification. Controlled comparisons using the same completed test values quantify the effect of aggregation without recalculating all earlier H1--H4 statistics.

\subsection{Original-Space and Incomplete-Data Models}

K-means and sampled partitioning around medoids (PAM) were evaluated for $k=2,\ldots,8$. PAM uses CLARA-style sampling, with three samples of 400 discovery patients and BUILD/SWAP optimization within each sample. Gaussian mixtures with full or tied covariances were evaluated for $k=1,\ldots,8$. Direct HDBSCAN used minimum cluster sizes 100 and 300 with \texttt{min\_samples}=20. A UMAP comparator used three dimensions, 15 or 50 neighbors, and \texttt{min\_dist}=0 before applying the same HDBSCAN settings. Validation assignments use the fitted centers, medoids, mixture probabilities, or UMAP transform and HDBSCAN approximate prediction.

Within each representation and method, partition/density models were selected by discovery silhouette and Gaussian mixtures by discovery BIC, requiring convergence where applicable and at least 2\% discovery membership in each group. Silhouette was computed in the corresponding original representation, including for UMAP-derived labels. Discovery silhouettes use up to 2{,}000 sampled observations; validation silhouettes use all available validation assignments. Noise is excluded from silhouette and coverage is reported separately. Scores from different feature geometries are not a common performance scale. Searching $k\geq2$ by silhouette cannot establish that groups exist relative to a one-population model.

Incomplete-data mixtures were fitted directly to the 14 transformed tests, integrating unobserved coordinates in an observed-data EM likelihood. The restricted families were Gaussian, Student-$t$ with fixed degrees of freedom 4 or 10, and contaminated Gaussian with a fixed 90\% core and tenfold covariance inflation for the remaining 10\%. Each family used $k=1,\ldots,8$, two initializations, a 120-iteration limit, a log-likelihood change tolerance of $10^{-5}$ per observation, and a dispersion-matrix eigenvalue floor of 0.02. For the $t$ family this is a scale-matrix floor. Mean filling was used only for initialization. These Python implementations were numerically tested; they are restricted adaptations, not reproductions of the complete \textit{MixtureMissing} package \parencite{tong2025mixturemissing}.

BIC, entropy-penalized ICL, convergence, validation observed-data log likelihood, and posterior membership uncertainty were recorded. Selected fits require convergence and at least 2\% discovery membership per component. Likelihood criteria are compared only within the same 14-test representation. These continuous distributions approximate bounded and discrete scores; extra components may model ceilings, skewness or tails rather than distinct patient types.

\subsection{Independent Refitting, Consensus and Cluster Tendency}

Twenty stochastic imputations were generated using bounded Bayesian posterior draws with ten chained-equations iterations per imputation. K-means and sampled PAM were independently fitted at fixed $k=2,3,4$ after refitting domain scaling for each draw. For this ensemble PAM used two samples of 300 patients. Every fit assigned the same 1{,}457 validation patients. Mean pairwise ARI and its empirical 5th--95th percentiles summarize assignment agreement across draws; these percentiles are descriptive, not confidence intervals, because partition pairs share fits. A separate k-means discovery-silhouette screen searched $k=2,\ldots,8$ in each draw.

Fifty 80\% subsamples of discovery patients refitted preprocessing, imputation, scaling and fixed three-group k-means. A separate 50-run initialization experiment varied clustering seeds with the input fixed. Held-out ARI and matched per-cluster Jaccard distinguish patient-sampling uncertainty from initialization. Mean and KNN imputation, no clipping, stricter test availability and independently fitted validation partitions provide additional sensitivity checks.

Average-linkage co-assignment, greedy mean-NMI and generic NMF aggregation were compared as descriptive summaries of held-out assignment vectors, following the ensemble question raised by \textcite{tomo2026ensemble}. The native implementations are not an exact replication of that study. Consensus formed on validation assignments is not itself a separately validated predictive model. Collapsed groups and nonconvergence are reported rather than hidden.

PCA/dip diagnostics were supplemented by a matched-size screen using 1{,}000 discovery patients and 100 single-Gaussian-copula reference datasets. Empirical discrete margins and resampled joint missingness masks were retained; preprocessing, imputation and the k-means $k=2,\ldots,8$ screen were repeated for each reference dataset. Tail fractions compare the observed maximum silhouette and PC1 dip statistic with their reference distributions. This is descriptive fitted-reference calibration: missingness masks are sampled independently of simulated values, and selection across all model families is not repeated. A separated two-group positive control checks basic recovery. Neither this screen nor a projection-based modality test establishes clinical subtypes \parencite{neville2026clusterability}.

\subsection{Patient Trajectories and Reproducibility}

The longitudinal subset requires at least three visits with seven or more observed tests, at least three distinct times, a 0.25--5 year span and constant recorded age at injury. The frozen index-discovery preprocessing, imputer and robust-space PC1 produce a longitudinal cognitive score, oriented toward better performance and divided by its index-discovery standard deviation. Time is elapsed years from the first sufficiently observed visit. Patient membership in discovery or validation remains unchanged.

A Gaussian linear trajectory mixture with one to four classes integrates patient random intercepts and slopes, sharing their covariance and the residual variance across classes. Three initializations and up to 300 EM iterations were used. Discovery BIC selects among converged fits whose expected membership is at least 5\% in every class. The one-class fit provides a continuous random-effects reference. Patient subsampling is conditional on selection of more than one class. This modest model examines observed course rather than causal recovery; it does not implement nonlinear trajectories or the published multivariate contaminated longitudinal model. Recent longitudinal comparisons motivate separating initial level from rate of change \parencite{julia2026longitudinal}.

Analyses used Python 3.12.14 with fixed dependency versions, reproducible random seeds and the model grids specified above. Eight numerical tests checked reference likelihoods, missing-data recovery, EM monotonicity, preprocessing direction, medoids, integrated trajectory likelihood and the ensemble objective. No independent rehabilitation outcome is available; predictive clinical utility is therefore untested.
