# Recent clustering research fitted to the ABI thesis

Prepared 15 October 2026. Eligible article publication window: **15 October 2025–15 October 2026**, inclusive.

Implementation follow-up: [executed results and limitations](clustering_results_2026.md). This review preserves the reasoning written before those analyses.

**Recommendation:** extend the existing analysis with original-space clusterability checks, robust mixture models for incomplete data, and independently refitted multiple-imputation ensembles. Investigate recovery trajectories as a separate patient-level question. The objective is to determine whether meaningful subgroups exist beyond global severity; a defensible result may still be a continuum.

This is a targeted research assessment, not an exhaustive systematic review. Seven published, peer-reviewed research/software articles are included below. Dates were checked against publisher pages or article PDFs, using online publication rather than issue year where available. Tutorials, reviews, preprints, conference abstracts and package release announcements are not used as the evidential basis. A recent article may implement older statistical methods; the date restriction applies to the cited articles, not the invention date of each algorithm.

**What the current work already establishes**

The README, thesis chapters, notebook source and saved result tables describe 17,406 eligible assessments from 7,285 patients, with 14 tests aggregated into six cognitive domains and ten imputation methods. These are existing project results, not newly recalculated estimates. The input spreadsheets have local changes, so the next analysis must record its input versions and verify cohort counts before relying on these outputs.

The main methodological signals are:

- The MICE labels have silhouette 0.526 in the selected UMAP embedding but 0.079 in the original robust-scaled domain space. This motivates testing the geometry before projection.
- Overall core Jaccard is 0.509–0.630 across methods, and the selected cluster count varies from three to five. A stable ordering is easier to defend than discrete phenotypes.
- H2's mean ARI of 0.710 measures assignment agreement under a MICE-trained scaler, UMAP and fixed three-cluster k-means model. It does not measure independent discovery of the same structure.
- The GMM baseline searches only two through six components; BIC improves through six. Neither the one-component alternative nor a clear interior optimum was evaluated.
- Most resampling treats assessments as independent. The saved longitudinal summary identifies 5,206 patients with repeated assessments, but label changes alone cannot distinguish recovery from measurement or assignment uncertainty.
- Only 53.1% of assessments are complete across all 14 tests. The missingness table reports variable-level missingness from 0.7% to 27.8% among the retained tests. The 50% filtering threshold is not the observed missingness rate of every retained variable.

**How to use these findings in this project**

1. **Test the unembedded data before searching for additional clusters.** Use one prespecified index assessment per patient for the primary cross-sectional experiment. Fit preprocessing on discovery patients. Examine cluster tendency in six-domain space, with the 14-test representation as a sensitivity analysis. Pair the projection-based tests with original-space separation and simulated continuous-severity reference datasets. A small p-value, a large silhouette after UMAP, and a clinically meaningful phenotype are different claims.

2. **Add a robust missing-data model as the main new discovery branch.** Start with normal, t, skew-t and contaminated-normal families on the 14 direction-aligned, standardized test variables with missing entries retained. This avoids constructing partly observed domain averages whose meaning changes with which tests were administered. Summarize fitted groups using the same six domains afterward. Use the imputed six-domain representation for a companion comparison. Evaluate one through eight components, with ten as a boundary sensitivity if needed; these are proposed search limits, not literature-derived optima. A single skewed population may be approximated by several mixture components, so component count is not synonymous with phenotype count.

3. **Extend H2 to actual refitted discovery.** Generate multiple stochastic imputations under one coherent model, refit the clustering for each completed dataset, then aggregate partitions and quantify uncertainty. The ten existing imputation algorithms remain a separate method-sensitivity dimension. Begin with a fixed candidate cluster count per ensemble and k-means/PAM in domain space. Study independently fitted HDBSCAN separately because noise and changing cluster counts complicate the comparison. Report the frequency of each selected cluster count, not just a single pooled label vector.

4. **Separate overall level from profile shape as an exploratory question.** The broad severity gradient may obscure relative domain weaknesses. Compare clustering of absolute domain scores with clustering after removing a training-derived general-severity direction. This residual-profile experiment is our proposed adaptation to this dataset, not a technique validated for ABI by the seven articles. Removing severity can also remove genuine signal or create constraints. Retain the absolute profiles, test against an appropriate continuous-factor null, and avoid treating residual clusters as confirmed phenotypes.

5. **Model recovery separately from cognitive state.** Use patient ID and elapsed time to study baseline level and subsequent change. Compare a modest distance/feature approach with a mixed-model trajectory approach. The choice depends on the number and spacing of visits, test coverage and dropout. Two assessments cannot reliably establish a nonlinear recovery class. This extension addresses a different estimand from the original assessment-level severity tiers and should receive its own results section.

The simplest useful benchmark is therefore **PAM and robust mixtures in the measured feature space, alongside the existing UMAP+HDBSCAN reference**. The most valuable uncertainty extension is **multiple-imputation ensembles with patient-grouped validation**. Deep clustering and incomplete multi-view networks are lower priorities: this dataset has only 14 retained tests, and six domain aggregates are not automatically six independently informative modalities.

**A concrete prerequisite found in the notebook source**

NB5's `compute_domain_scores` clips at the 1st and 99th percentiles, reverses `ATMTA`, standardizes individual variables, and then aggregates by domain. NB14's `run_pipeline` and complete-case branch instead weight the raw columns before domain standardization; the inspected blocks omit the variable-level clipping, direction reversal and standardization. Thus those sensitivity branches do not implement the same domain construction. Reuse one fitted preprocessing function before interpreting differences as effects of imputation or clustering. The existing frequency weights should also be described as administration-frequency weights, not psychometric reliability.

**What would justify a stronger clustering claim**

A credible subgroup should recur when patients, imputations and initialization vary; retain a recognizable profile in the original features; resist explanation by missingness patterns, test limits or overall severity alone; and show useful replication or association with an independent endpoint. No single numerical threshold establishes all of these.

Use patients as the resampling unit, refit preprocessing inside discovery folds, and reserve validation patients before parameter selection. Compare a continuous-severity model with severity plus cluster membership on a prespecified outcome measured independently of the clustering tests. The current clinical-unit association and time-since-injury comparison provide context, not established treatment utility. If there is no suitable external outcome in the extract, report that validation gap explicitly.

A failure to find reproducible categorical structure is still informative. It would strengthen the use of continuous scores and, where operationally useful, clearly labeled severity bands. It would not prove that no subtypes can exist in other features, time windows or populations.

**Search and verification record**

Searches covered recent ABI/neuropsychological phenotyping, clusterability, UMAP distortions, incomplete-data mixture models, multiple-imputation ensembles and longitudinal clustering. Final claims were checked against journal pages or the publisher's article PDF. Older search hits, including the February 2025 chronic-TBI phenotype study, were excluded from the evidence set. A May 2026 TBI poster and a May 2026 k-POD conference-supplement abstract were not promoted to full research-article evidence. Recent package documentation was used for discovery only. No patient-level data were sent to external services.

This document records the literature assessment and original experiment design. The subsequent implementation, numerical results and deviations are documented in the [results report](clustering_results_2026.md). No superiority claim follows from the literature review alone.
