\begin{abstract}[]
\addchaptertocentry{\abstractname}
Neuropsychological assessments after acquired brain injury (ABI) are often incomplete. This thesis examines whether clustering recovers reproducible cognitive structure while accounting for sensitivity to missing-data handling and feature construction.

The historical analysis includes 17{,}406 assessments from 7{,}285 patients, using 14 tests across six domains and ten imputation methods. Its UMAP+HDBSCAN solution yields three descriptive severity bands. A fixed-reference comparison across imputations gives mean adjusted Rand index (ARI) 0.710. Clinical-unit association is small, without clear added patient-grouped predictive value beyond the broad diagnostic grouping. These results do not establish sharply discrete phenotypes.

An exploratory extension uses the earliest eligible assessment per patient, with 5{,}828 discovery and 1{,}457 validation patients. Discovery-fitted preprocessing supports original-space clustering, observed-data robust mixtures, 20 independently refitted stochastic imputations, 50 patient subsamples and mixed-effects trajectories.

Two-group k-means is selected in all 20 imputation screens, with mean pairwise validation ARI 0.959; three-group k-means gives ARI 0.935. Changing from robust to equal-domain scaling nevertheless changes three-group assignments substantially (ARI 0.243), and test availability differs between groups. Robust $t$ mixtures improve observed-data fit but prefer eight components, the search boundary. Among 2{,}013 longitudinally eligible patients, multi-class trajectories introduce groups below the specified 5\% size threshold.

The evidence supports reproducible cognitive score groupings conditional on representation and imputation assumptions. Distinct clinical phenotypes and treatment utility remain unconfirmed. Independent outcomes and external cohorts are needed before clinical use. CogDash presents both analysis designs for research; analysis code is available at \url{https://github.com/zkunos/thesis_project}.
\end{abstract}
